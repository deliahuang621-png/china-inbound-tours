China Visa-Free Playbook for Tour Operators

Official-source verified 2026-10-01 Sources: National Immigration Administration (NIA) of China, policy interpretations dated 2026-02-17 and 2026-08-20. Links at the bottom of this page.

Five visa-free channels matter for inbound group travel to China. A route concept is only sellable if its geography, day count and client nationality all fit inside one of these channels. This page is the lookup table — built for product managers, not lawyers.

The five channels

ChannelEligible nationalitiesMax stayAllowed areaKey conditions
A. Unilateral 30-day waiver
(NIA, 50 countries)		 Europe (35): France, Germany, Italy, Spain, Switzerland, Ireland, Hungary, Austria, Belgium, Netherlands, Poland, Portugal, Greece, Nordics, Baltics, Balkans, Russia, UK, etc.
Oceania: Australia, New Zealand
Asia: Japan, South Korea, Brunei, Saudi Arabia, Oman, Kuwait, Bahrain
Americas: Brazil, Argentina, Chile, Peru, Uruguay, Canada
Not included: USA, Mexico		 30 days (counted from 00:00 the day after entry) Nationwide, all purposes incl. tourism, business, exchange visits Ordinary passport. The default channel for A-list nationalities.
B. 240-hour transit waiver
(NIA, 57 countries)		 The 50 above plus USA, Canada, Mexico, plus Indonesia, Vietnam, Kyrgyzstan, Qatar, UAE (total 57) 240 hours (10 days) Enter via any of 65 designated ports in 24 provinces; move freely within the permitted areas across those provinces Confirmed onward ticket (fixed date + seat) to a third country or region — returning to the departure country does not qualify. Hong Kong and Macau count as third regions. Study, work and news reporting are excluded. School study camps cannot use this channel.
C. Hainan 30-day waiver
(NIA, 61 countries)		 The A-list plus USA, Canada, Mexico, Ukraine, Belarus, Serbia, Thailand, Philippines, Indonesia, Vietnam, Kazakhstan, UAE, Qatar and others (full list in the official source) 30 days Hainan province only Tourism, business, visits, family, medical, exhibitions, sports. Work and study excluded.
F1. HK/Macau group → Guangdong
(NIA, no nationality restriction)		 Citizens of any country with diplomatic relations with China 6 days All of Guangdong province; enter/exit as a group (2+) organized by a HK/Macau-registered travel agency through open ports of the 10 eligible cities Group must travel as a unit (group in, group out). The only fully visa-free channel for US student groups into Guangdong.
F2. HK/Macau group → Hainan
(NIA, no nationality restriction)		 Same as F1 6 days Hainan province only Same group mechanics as F1. Backup channel for short Hainan products.
G. Mutual exemption treaties
(MFA consular service list)		 29 countries incl. Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand 30 days (max 90 days per 180) Nationwide For G-list nationalities this stacks with every other channel.
Deliberately not used in any of my concepts: ad-hoc reliance on the 24-hour transit rule, and any port list not published by NIA. Every channel above links to an official source below.

Worked examples for product teams

  • US school group, 13 days, STEM theme: Channel A ❌ (US not on the 50-country list) → use Channel C (Hainan 30-day) for a Hainan-based camp, or Channel B limited to 10 days, or plan for full visas. My Hainan study camp concept is built on Channel C.
  • US group, 6 days, Guangdong: Channel F1 ✅ — group organized via HK/Macau, any nationality, all of Guangdong.
  • Canadian group, 14 days, nationwide: Channel A ✅ — Canada is on the 50-country list.
  • Malaysian or Singaporean group: Channel G ✅ 30 days nationwide, stacking with F1 for Guangdong extensions.
  • US foodie couple, 10 days, Sichuan + Yunnan: Channel B ✅ — Chengdu, Chongqing and the Yunnan corridor all sit inside the permitted areas; pair with an onward ticket to Bangkok or Hong Kong.
